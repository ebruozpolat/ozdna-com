DT 5.0 · Art. 9 — risk management

Classification: workload → risk tier

Risk management under Article 9 is continuous and lifecycle-wide. DT 5.0 places each workload on a tier and makes the routing decision that follows from it — so the risk posture is computed, not assumed.

TIER · LOW

Cheapest sufficient model

Routine work clears on the most economical model that meets the bar. No premium paid for risk that isn't there.

TIER · ELEVATED

Stronger model, logged

Higher-stakes work takes a stronger model and is recorded for traceability.

TIER · HIGH

Escalated to the Council

Decisions that carry regulatory weight are routed to multi-model oversight before they stand.

Council · Art. 14 — human oversight

A veto, in software

Article 14 asks for oversight that can stop a system and that guards against over-reliance on automation. The Council implements exactly that: several models vote, any one can veto, and a fail-closed judge holds the decision when the vote is unsafe.

Over-reliance on automation is designed out: the safe default is a stop, and a human always has the last word on a held decision.
